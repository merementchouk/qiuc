** Motivation

We saw that spin-based computing treats data-processing tasks in its own, quite distinctive way. Some tasks that may require elaborate conventional algorithms have surprisingly simple form, for example, finding such spin configurations \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\) that deliver minimum of \(Q^2(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\), where \(Q(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\) is not a particularly complex function.

At the same time, we saw that finding such a minimum is not a simple task either. We looked at a family of local search algorithms (\(k\)-opt local search) guaranteeing that there are no better configurations within Hamming distance \(k\) and came to a rather discouraging conclusion. While the complexity of these algorithms grows exponentially with \(k\), they are still prone to ending up in a local trap: the state is an algorithm's stable state but does not yield minimum of \(Q^2(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\).

How common or artificial may be tasks with such traps? It is conjectured that
[[./topics/2026-09-14-dipath.html][finding path in a bipartite directed graph]] may be hard for local search, because for any \(k\), the correcting spin transformation may require inverting more than \(k\) spins. At the same time, that same problem can be routinely solved using, say, the breadth-first search (BFS).

Hamming distance-bounded search seems like a natural limitation. Does this mean that we have an inherent limitation on "native" implementations of spin-based computations?

It is, therefore, important that relaxation-based dynamical Ising machines (R-DIMs), in particular the one driven by the V-2 model, are not subject to Hamming-distance bound. Implementing the spin-based path finding algorithm is not a challenge for the V-2-driven Ising machine (still a hypothesis at the time of the meeting).

Considering the relative simplicity of the equations of motion governing the V-2 model, such freedom from the Hamming-distance bound appears, on the one hand, surprising and, on the other hand, illustrating the hidden computational significance of dynamical systems. Therefore, we will consider the V-2 model dynamics in detail to give a precise formulation of the unbounded search supported by the V-2 model and make it bulletproof.

** Phase space of relaxed spins

Within the family of relaxation-based dynamical Ising machines (R-DIMs), relaxed spins are introduced by representing binary spins of the canonical Ising model by the pair \(\psi = \left( \sigma, X \right)\), where \(\sigma \in \left\lbrace -1, 1 \right\rbrace\) represents the binary spin, and \(X \in [-1, 1]\) is the continuous component of the relaxed spin.

The phase space of a single relaxed spin is convenient to define as \(\mathcal{R} = \left\lbrace -1, 1 \right\rbrace \times [-1, 1] / \sim\), with the equivalence relation
\[
(\sigma, 1) \sim (-\sigma, -1).
\]

** Global properties of relaxed spin functions

We characterize states, relaxed spins configurations \(\boldsymbol{\psi} = \left( \psi_1, \ldots, \psi_N \right) \in \mathcal{R}^N\), by functions on states, \(\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{\psi})\).  Requiring that these functions are continuous on the network phase space, and, in particular, are consistent with the equivalence relation, imposes constraints on their form.

We consider these constraints for the example of the R-DIM's relaxed cut function

\[
\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) = \mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{m,n} A_{m,n} \sigma_m \sigma_n \Phi(X_m - X_n),
\]

where

\[
\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{m,n} A_{m,n}\left( 1 - \sigma_m \sigma_n \right) 
\]

is the original (unrelaxed) cut function, and \(\Phi(X)\) is the relaxed cut indicator. Generally, this indicator is only required to be continuous and satisfy \(\Phi(0) = 0\) and \(\Phi(\pm 2) = 1\). Also, without loss of generality, we can assume that the indicator is an even function \(\Phi(X) = \Phi(-X)\). However, requiring continuity on \(\mathcal{R}^N\) adds an additional nontrivial constraint.

To derive this constraint, we consider the case when one relaxed spin, say, with number \(i \in \left\lbrace 1, \ldots, N \right\rbrace\), is subject to the equivalence relation, that is \(X_i = 1\). Thus, the respective relaxed spin configuration, \(\boldsymbol{\psi} = \left( \boldsymbol{\sigma}, \mathbf{X} \right)\), is equivalent to another configuration, \(\boldsymbol{\psi} \sim \boldsymbol{\psi}'\), where \(\boldsymbol{\psi}' = \left( \boldsymbol{\sigma}', \mathbf{X}' \right)\), with \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}'\) and \(\mathbf{X}'\) obtained through the equivalence relation: \(\sigma'_i = -\sigma_i\) and \(X' _i = -1\). For the relaxed cut function, this implies that \(\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) = \mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\psi}')\). Using the expression for the relaxed cut function and cancelling terms that do not contain \(i\) (they are the same for both configurations), we find that the following equality must hold for an arbitrary set \(X_n\) with \(n \ne i\):

\[
\sum_{n} A_{i,n} \sigma_n \left[ 1 - \Phi(X_n + 1) - \Phi(X_n - 1) \right] = 0,
\]

where we have taken into account that the indicator function is even. Since the term in the brackets must vanish identically, we obtain that the relaxed cut indicator function must possess gliding symmetry, which we write in the form suitable for extending the indicator periodically to the whole real line with the period \(4\)

\[\Phi(X \pm 2) = 1 - \Phi(X).\]

A generalization to the case when multiple relaxed spins are subject to the equivalence relation is straightforward and yields the same result.

** V-2 model

The V-2 model is defined by \(\Phi(X) = |X|/2\), so that its relaxed cut function has the form


\[
\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) = \mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) + \frac{1}{4} \sum_{m,n} A_{m,n} \sigma_m \sigma_n |X_m - X_n|,
\]

and its dynamics on the phase space \(\mathcal{R}^N\) is governed by the equations of motion

\[
 \dot{X}_m = \sigma_m \sum_{n} A_{m,n} \sigma_n \mathrm{sgn}(X_m - X_n),
\]

where \(\mathrm{sgn}(X)\) is the sign function. This function is discontinuous at the origin, and this discontinuity is not innocuous. For example, while investigating the evolution of distribution functions (measures) on the phase space induced by the equations of motion, the discontinuity must be approached with care. However, for a broad class of problems, a simple convention \(\mathrm{sgn}(0) = 0\) provides sufficient and powerful tool for analyzing general computational capabilities of the V-2 model.

From the physical perspective, for instance, in view of realizations of the V-2 model in physical systems, discontinuities are natural to consider from regularization point of view: replacing the relaxed cut indicator by a function \(\Phi_\epsilon(X)\) with continuous derivative, such that \(\Phi_\epsilon(X) = \Phi(X)\)  for \(|X| > \epsilon\), where \(\epsilon > 0\) is the regularization scale. Without diving into the dynamics of the regularized V-2 model, it is worth noting that the validity of the simplified treatment of discontinuities results from continuity of the relaxed cut function \(\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\psi})\), our main interest in the computational content of the relaxed spin configuration represented by its /discrete/ component \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\), and the scale-free nature of the V-2 model equations of motion, which we will discuss in detail later.
